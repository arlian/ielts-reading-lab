# Writing Guide: High-Quality IELTS-Style Passages

## What Makes a Passage "Contextual"

Contextual passages feel like real magazine articles with concrete, verifiable details. Abstract passages feel like textbooks with theory and no examples.

### ❌ Abstract (avoid)
> "Forgetting is an active process. Modern neuroscience shows that the brain has dedicated mechanisms to weaken unused memories. This helps people think flexibly. For example, people who discard irrelevant details perform better on thinking tests."

**Problems:** 
- No specific people, places, or institutions named
- No dates or time references
- No numbers or statistics
- Pure theory with vague examples
- Sounds like a Wikipedia summary

### ✅ Contextual (use this approach)
> "In 2014, Dr. Rajesh Patel established a clinic in Jaipur, India, specialising in PTSD among refugees and accident survivors. His team found that 73% of patients experienced debilitating flashbacks. After twelve weeks of treatment with propranolol, most patients reported a 60% reduction in flashback intensity. A 2017 study at McGill University in Montreal showed that memories become unstable when recalled, creating a window where the emotional tag can be rewritten."

**Strengths:**
- Named person (Dr. Rajesh Patel) and location (Jaipur, India)
- Specific year (2014) and timeframe (twelve weeks)
- Concrete statistics (73%, 60% reduction)
- Real drug name (propranolol)
- Real institution (McGill University)
- Grounded example with actual outcomes

## Checklist: Every Passage Should Have

- [ ] **Specific dates / years** — "In 2019", "Since 1995", "Over five years"
- [ ] **Named places** — city, country, region, building (e.g. "Tokyo", "the Amazon", "MIT", "a warehouse in Shanghai")
- [ ] **Real people or organizations** — names, titles, companies (e.g. "Dr. Sarah Chen", "BMW", "the Red Cross")
- [ ] **Numbers and statistics** — percentages, quantities, costs, measurements (e.g. "47% of respondents", "€2.3 million", "12,000 users", "three hours")
- [ ] **Case studies or real examples** — specific scenarios from real life, not hypotheticals (e.g. "In 2018, a small farm in Kenya…" not "Imagine a farmer…")
- [ ] **Practical outcomes or consequences** — what actually happened or what the real impact was
- [ ] **Concrete details** — actual method names, drug names, process steps (e.g. "propranolol", "reconsolidation therapy", not just "a treatment")

## How to Build a Contextual Passage

### Step 1: Pick a real or plausible situation
**Instead of:** "Some people forget things"  
**Do this:** "In 2014, Dr. Rajesh Patel opened a clinic in Jaipur treating trauma survivors"

### Step 2: Add specific details
- Where exactly? (city, institution name)
- Who? (names, titles, roles)
- When? (year, duration, timeline)
- How many? (numbers, statistics, real measurements)
- What happened? (real outcomes, not theories)

### Step 3: Ground each claim in a concrete example
**Instead of:** "Memory training improves performance"  
**Do this:** "At McGill University in Montreal, a 2017 study found that when patients recalled traumas within six hours of taking propranolol, flashback intensity dropped 60% after twelve weeks"

### Step 4: Use real institutions and product names
- Real universities, companies, hospitals
- Real drugs, technologies, methodologies
- Real countries, cities, organizations
- Real studies with years and authors when possible

## Example: Before and After

### Before (Abstract)
> "Artificial intelligence systems are becoming more common. Some experts worry about safety. Companies are developing new safeguards. The technology has many applications in different fields."

- No specifics, no evidence, no grounding

### After (Contextual)
> "In 2021, DeepMind released a study showing that AI systems trained on large language models could generate text faster than humans could read it. OpenAI's GPT-3, deployed in 2020, was used by 10,000 businesses within its first year. However, in 2023, a team at MIT found that 18% of GPT-3 outputs contained factually incorrect claims. In response, OpenAI began requiring a human review step for high-stakes applications, increasing processing time by 40%."

- Named companies (DeepMind, OpenAI, MIT)
- Specific years (2021, 2020, 2023)
- Concrete numbers (10,000 businesses, 18%, 40%)
- Real products (GPT-3)
- Actual research findings and consequences

## When Creating Tests

1. **Choose a contextual subject** — a real institution, program, person, or event
2. **Ground the passage in time and place** — specific dates, locations, names
3. **Include measurable details** — statistics, costs, durations, participant numbers
4. **Show real consequences** — what happened, what changed, what people learned
5. **Make questions test real comprehension** — not just theory recall, but understanding of concrete scenarios

This makes passages engaging, memorable, and genuinely educational — like reading a quality magazine article rather than a generic textbook.
